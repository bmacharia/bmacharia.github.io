_“Sawadee ka,”_ I mumble, and stop.

One week in Thailand and all I’ve done is repeating my smile, nod, freeze sequence. The last time I felt this incompetent in a language I needed was in 2017 just before heading to Chile for an exchange semester.

Back then, I will-powered my way through 5,000 flashcards, a crash course in spaced repetition, fueled by my fear of failing my classes. It worked, but it was chaotic and I didn’t know whether it was really the best way to learn a language. I had no framework, no structure, no feedback.

Nine years later, and a lot has changed.

I’ve studied how adults learn, interviewed polyglots, reviewed second-language research, and watched AI reshape what’s possible. We now have tools that adapt to us. You can build a language system around your life, constraints, and goals.

This article shows you how.

I built a 12-week Thai learning system from scratch (no Duolingo, rote drills, or “try Youtube” advice). Instead, I used methods that work:

- Comprehensible input to flood the brain with almost-understandable content
    
- Inductive grammar to spot patterns without memorizing rules
    
- Errorfull learning to practice and get feedback
    
- AI as tutor and role-play partner
    

I’ll share the exact research-level prompt I used to generate a personalized learning roadmap, my three-phase project plan, and the tools I trust (and the ones I avoid).

You’ll also see how I turned a vague intention—“I want to speak Thai”—into a system I actually follow. **Whether you’re starting fresh or restarting a stalled language, this piece gives you the evidence-based personalized plan to learn faster and smarter.**

## The Prompt You Can Copy to Get Your Master Plan

If you ask an AI, “How do I learn Thai?”, it will give you the internet’s greatest hits: “Try Duolingo” or “memorize common phrases.” None of that is wrong, but none of it is fully aligned with how adult brains actually acquire a new language.

The prompt below fixes that. It hard-codes the evidence-based principles [I explored before](https://evakeiffenheim.substack.com/p/why-your-language-classes-failed) (sources below this articles) and directs your LLM of choice to build a plan that is realistic, personalized, and actually doable.

All you need to do is copy it, replace the [brackets] with your details, paste it into ChatGPT Deep Research, Claude 4.5 Opus, or Gemini 3.5 Deep Research (I tested all three so you don’t have to), and let it build your roadmap.

The prompt:

```
# Role
You are an expert researcher and practitioner at the intersection of second language acquisition, AI-assisted learning, and edtech evaluation. You combine knowledge from applied linguistics, cognitive science, and learning design to produce evidence-based, practical recommendations.

# Context
You are advising a motivated learner who wants to learn [TARGET LANGUAGE].

Learner Profile:
Current Level: [Describe your level, e.g., “Absolute zero” or “I did Duolingo for a month but can’t speak”].
Language Background: [List your native language and any other languages you speak proficiently].
Motivation & Timeline: [Describe why you are learning and your deadline, e.g., “I am moving to Tokyo in 6 months” or “I want to speak with my partner’s family by March 2026”].
Specific Interest:  [List 2-3 themes you generally like to talk about (this will inform some of the role-playing scenarios) e.g. cultural history, sci-fi books, cooking].

The Definition of Success:
The learner wants to achieve [Target Proficiency Level, e.g., Conversational Fluency / B1 Level] within [Timeframe, e.g., 3 months]. Specifically, they want to be able to:
[Specific Scenario 1: e.g., Order vegetarian food and ask about ingredients]
[Specific Scenario 2: e.g., Discuss my job in Tech Sales with peers]
[Specific Scenario 3: e.g., Navigate public transport and taxis]

# Timeline & Logistics 
The plan must fit within these realistic time constraints:
Commute/Passive Time: [e.g., every weekday 30 mins per day walking/driving - audio only].
Daily Review: [e.g., every weekday - 10 mins morning coffee break - phone only].
Deep Work: [e.g., 1x 60-minute sessions every Sunday morning at a desk].
Total Duration: [e.g., 16 weeks].
The learner is willing to use: [e.g., Anki, Spotify, Netflix, “Open to buying 1-2 programs/apps”].

# Constraints & Preferences
The learner values efficiency and evidence-based strategies. Avoid “busywork” (e.g., generic streak apps without substance). Efficiency and evidence-based means:

Do not use traditional classroom methods (rote memorization, isolated grammar drills, or gamified busywork). Instead, structure the entire learning roadmap around the 3-Pillar Framework:

Pillar 1 (Flood the Soil): Focus on Comprehensible Input (95-98% understanding) to lower the affective filter.
Pillar 2 (Grow Grammar): Use “Grammar by Induction” (noticing patterns in context) and occasional explanations rather than memorizing rules.
Pillar 3 (Compost Errors): Use “Errorful Learning” (Output → Feedback → Repair) to turn mistakes into long-term retention.

If the target language uses a different writing system (e.g., Japanese, Thai, Arabic, Russian) and the user is a beginner, consider whether the script is relevant for the desired learning outcome, and if yes, include a specific strategy for learning the script alongside the pillars.

# Task
Design a Language Learning Roadmap. Consider the strengths of Generative AI and Existing Evidence-based Tools (Apps/Courses) to assign the best tool for the job.

For each Pillar, specify:
-> The Strategy: What is the cognitive goal?
-> The “Hybrid” Tool Stack:
Where to use Existing Tools: (e.g., “Use Pimsleur for audio foundations” or “Use Lingvist for high-frequency vocab”).
-> Where to use GenAI: (e.g., “Use Gemini to generate stories about the user's specific interest” or “Use Claude to roleplay a specific scenario close to the desired learning outcome”).
The Routine: A concrete daily protocol mapped against the specified time slots. Ensure the activity fits the device (e.g., no typing exercises during “Phone only” time).

# Output Format
1. The Hybrid Tech Stack
A bulleted list of the 3-4 essential tools selected for this learner, explaining why each was chosen over the others (e.g., “Why Anki instead of a notebook?”).
2. The 3-Month Roadmap (Weekly View)
Break down the [Timeline] into phases.
Phase 1 (Foundation): Focus on...
Phase 2 (Growth): Focus on...
Phase 3 (Fluency): Focus on...
3. The AI Prompt Library
Provide 3 exact prompts the learner needs to copy/paste to make the AI parts of the plan work.

Prompt A (Input Generator): For generating reading material at the right level.
Prompt B (Grammar Detective): For understanding rules via context.
Prompt C (The Simulator): For roleplaying the specific scenarios listed in the Context.

# Meta-Instructions
Balance: Do not force AI where it isn’t needed. If for example, a podcast is better for the commute than an AI conversation with gemini, suggest the podcast.
Efficiency: The plan must fit the Available Time.
Actionable: End with “Day One Instructions”—the exact first thing the learner should do after reading this response.
```

You’ll need to reflect a bit on what you want to be able to _do_ in the language and when you want to do it—but that small upfront effort unlocks a tailored plan that aligns with your real life, not an imaginary one.

Once you have your AI-generated plan, read through it and check whether the recommendations make sense for _you_ Look at the suggested tools and platforms and ask the only question that matters: **Will I actually use this?**

Even the most evidence-based method won’t help you if it never gets opened. Give the model feedback. Tell it what feels right, what feels off, and what needs adjusting.

Below is the prompt exactly as I used it. You don’t need to rewrite it. Just fill in your date and paste.

```
Now create a copy-ready language learning project plan starting [insert date] that details exactly what to do each day for the first 4 weeks, based on the schedule, tools, goals, and methods you researched above. This should include a day-by-day schedule, tool setup checklist, and relevant app or media links to bookmark. I want to paste the output directly into Google Docs or Word.
```

## How I Used This Prompt to Build My Own Thai Learning System (So You Can Do the Same)

To show you this isn’t theoretical, here’s how I used the prompt to build my own 12-week Thai learning system in under 15 minutes.

The model returned a structured roadmap broken into three phases—foundation → growth → fluency—plus a hybrid tool stack and a day-by-day protocol that fit my real schedule.

It even integrated Thai script without overwhelming me (something every beginner underestimates). Below is the exact setup I used, followed by the model’s output. You can swap in your details and generate your own version in minutes.

```
# Role

You are an expert researcher and practitioner at the intersection of second language acquisition, AI-assisted learning, and edtech evaluation. You combine knowledge from applied linguistics, cognitive science, and learning design to produce evidence-based, practical recommendations.


# Context

You are advising a motivated learner who wants to learn Thai.

Learner Profile:
Current Level: absolute zero.
Language Background: German native, English C2; Spanish B2; Hindi A1.
Motivation & Timeline: I live in Thailand for the next 6 months and want to be able to speak to locals. 
Specific Interest:  cultural history, DJing, buddhism, philosophy. 

The Definition of Success:
The learner wants to achieve conversational fluency within 3 month]. Specifically, she wants to be able to:
Engage in basic social conversation (e.g., greetings, emotions, simple daily updates).
Give feedback or instructions  at massage studio (e.g., “less hard please,” “stay on this spot”).
Order vegetarian meals restaurants and cafes (“one iced americano,”  “little spicy” “I’m vegetarian,” etc.).
Manage payment and logistics (“what’s the total?” “I’ll pay by card” “I don’t need a bag, thank you”).
Give short directions to taxi drivers (“turn left,” “straight ahead”).
Read and pronounce Thai script at a basic level.

# Timeline & Logistics 
The plan must fit within these realistic time constraints:
Commute/Passive Time: 4x week 30 mins per day walking on a treadmill (phone for reading and watching available, no speaking possible).
Daily Review: every day 20 minutes for Anki-style flashcard or review 
Deep Work: 1x 60-minute sessions every Saturday at a desk 
Total Duration: [e.g., 12 weeks].
The learner is willing to use: [e.g., Anki, Spotify, Netflix,  and has bought already ThaiPod101 and can buy up to 1 more programme/app].

# Constraints & Preferences

The learner values efficiency and evidence-based strategies. Avoid “busywork” (e.g., generic streak apps without substance). Efficiency and evidence-based means:
Do not use traditional classroom methods (rote memorization, isolated grammar drills, or gamified busywork). Instead, structure the entire learning roadmap around the 3-Pillar Framework:

# Task
Design a Language Learning Roadmap. Consider the strengths of Generative AI and Existing Evidence-based Tools (Apps/Courses) to assign the best tool for the job. For each Pillar, specify:

-> The Strategy: What is the cognitive goal?
-> The “Hybrid” Tool Stack:
Where to use Existing Tools: (e.g., “Use Pimsleur for audio foundations” or “Use Lingvist for high-frequency vocab”).

-> Where to use GenAI: (e.g., “Use Gemini to generate stories about the user’s specific interest” or “Use Claude to roleplay a specific scenario close to the desired learning outcome”).

The Routine: A concrete daily protocol mapped against the specified time slots. Ensure the activity fits the device (e.g., no typing exercises during “Phone only” time).

# Output Format

1. The Hybrid Tech Stack

A bulleted list of the 3-4 essential tools selected for this learner, explaining why each was chosen over the others (e.g., “Why Anki instead of a notebook?”).

2. The 3-Month Roadmap (Weekly View)

Break down the [Timeline] into phases.

Phase 1 (Foundation): Focus on...

Phase 2 (Growth): Focus on...

3. The AI Prompt Library
```

### How the Different AI Models Performed (So You Know What to Expect Before You Try)

Because I was nerdy-curious (and because model choice really matters for complex tasks), I ran the same prompt through all three major “deep research” models. I didn’t change a word.

Here’s the short version:

- **Gemini 3.5 Deep Research** did the best job integrating AI into the actual practice (roleplay, multimodal input, scenario work). [See the result here](https://gemini.google.com/share/c7954ba28d79).
    
- **Claude 4.5 Opus (Research + Extended Thinking)** surfaced the strongest tool recommendations and built the most structured roadmap. [See the result here](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1kI_lnSEHB2_1Zzi_EnkMC8Y6NlmD5WeG/edit?usp=sharing&ouid=105973963117325193836&rtpof=true&sd=true).
    
- **ChatGPT Deep Research** delivered the clearest synthesis—slightly less detailed, but easy to use. [See the result here](https://chatgpt.com/share/692d24fd-8dd8-8003-b981-5d7143cdccfd).
    

You don’t need three outputs like I did, one is enough. But model choice does change the quality and usefulness of the plan.
